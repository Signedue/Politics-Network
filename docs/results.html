01 / Why raw agreement is not enough

Observed similarity versus the vote-preserving null

Observed

0.741

Null expectation

0.747

Interpretation

Raw agreement is not enough

Politicians agree on roughly 74% of votes they both participate in, but the vote-preserving null expects almost the same average level of agreement.

Key evidence

Observed mean0.741
Null expectation0.747

What we can say

Much of the high raw voting agreement is already expected from the outcome distributions of parliamentary votes. Pair-specific voting structure therefore needs to be evaluated relative to the null baseline rather than from raw similarity alone.

What we cannot say

This does not imply that parliamentary voting is random or that politicians do not differ systematically. The result is conditional on this null model, which preserves participation and each vote's For, Imod, and Hverken totals while randomizing which participating politicians cast those positions.

Interpretation

Edges represent excess voting agreement

The provisional network keeps pairwise relationships that exceed the vote-preserving baseline and pass the selected statistical criterion.

Key evidence150 eligible politicians · 3,086 edges · 6 components · 117 politicians in the giant component

What we can say

Edges require at least 40 shared participated votes, positive excess similarity, and BH FDR q < 0.05. Their weight is excess similarity, so the network captures more than simply counting same-way votes.

What we cannot say

An edge is not evidence of friendship, political alignment, or strategic coordination. It represents a statistical relationship in observed parliamentary voting during the analysed period.

Interpretation

The community solution is reproducible across seeds

Across 20 weighted Louvain runs with different fixed random seeds, the algorithm repeatedly returned a very similar partition.

Key evidence9 communities in every run · Mean pairwise NMI 0.9623 · Median pairwise NMI 0.9629

What we can say

The detected community structure is highly consistent across these runs. Changing the algorithm's random seed generally produces very similar partitions.

What we cannot say

Stability does not establish that nine communities are the uniquely correct division of the network. It shows that this network and this method repeatedly recover similar partitions.

04 / Compared with null

Real Q0.1530

Null SD0.00229

Empirical p0.00990

Interpretation

Community structure exceeds the selected network null

The representative network has higher modularity than the degree-preserving null distribution used for comparison.

Key evidenceObserved Q 0.1530 · Null mean Q 0.0630 · Null SD 0.00229 · Empirical one-sided p 0.00990

What we can say

Under the selected baseline, the observed network contains stronger community structure than expected. The null preserves the node set and degree sequence, rewires edges, and randomly permutes observed weights across shuffled edges.

What we cannot say

This does not explain why communities exist or establish party strategy, government/opposition relations, or personal relationships. The conclusion is conditional on the chosen network definition and null model.

Interpretation

Voting communities overlap with — but do not reproduce — parties

The representative Louvain communities and formal party membership contain overlapping information without producing identical partitions.

Key evidenceCommunity ↔ party NMI 0.6449

What we can say

Party membership and detected voting communities overlap, but the voting network is not perfectly described by party labels alone.

What we cannot say

NMI does not explain why particular politicians or parties appear together or apart, nor does it establish that either partition is better or more meaningful. Those differences require direct examination of composition and voting patterns.

06 / Community composition

Communities contain both concentrated and mixed party composition

Interpretation

Party composition varies across the representative partition

Communities 0, 3, 4, and 5 are mixed, while communities 1 and 2 contain only DF and DD members respectively. Communities 6, 7, and 8 are one-member UFG isolates.

Key evidenceCommunity 0: 46 members and 6 parties · Community 3: 39 members and 4 parties · Community 4: 18 members and 3 parties · Community 5: 25 members and 3 parties

What we can say

The partition contains several communities with multiple party labels as well as single-party communities. This is consistent with overlap between voting communities and formal party membership without making them identical.

What we cannot say

Community IDs do not rank communities, and party composition alone does not establish ideology, motives, or causal relationships. The chart describes the selected network partition.

07 / Party-pair agreement

Pairwise excess similarity and filtered edges measure different things

Hover values include the party pair, metric value, eligible pair count, and Rule B edge count. Unavailable party pairs have no eligible comparison and are not treated as zero.

Interpretation

Pairwise agreement is not the same as a network edge

Mean excess similarity summarizes all eligible politician pairs in a party pair, while the Rule B edge fraction counts only pairs that also pass the existing statistical edge rule.

Key evidence8,794 eligible politician pairs · 3,086 Rule B edges

What we can say

The two views describe different stages of the validated workflow: continuous pairwise excess similarity versus the proportion retained as Rule B edges.

What we cannot say

Small eligible-pair counts make individual cells less informative, and neither metric explains why parties or politicians differ. Missing cells indicate unavailable eligible comparisons, not zero similarity.
