01 / Why raw agreement is not enough

Observed similarity versus the vote-preserving null

Observed

0.741

Null expectation

0.747

Why it mattersRaw agreement alone does not distinguish observed structure from agreement expected from vote-level outcomes.

What we see

Politicians agree on roughly 74% of votes they both participate in. Mean observed pairwise similarity is 0.741, compared with 0.747 under the vote-preserving null.

What it tells us

High raw agreement is not by itself evidence of a distinctive pairwise relationship. Much agreement is already expected from parliamentary vote outcomes, motivating comparison with the null baseline.

What it does not tell us

This does not show that voting is random or that politicians do not differ systematically. The null preserves participation and each vote's exact For, Imod, and Hverken totals while randomizing which participants cast them.

What we see

There are 150 eligible politicians, 3,086 validated edges, 6 connected components, and 117 politicians in the giant component. Edges require at least 40 shared participated votes, positive excess similarity, and BH FDR q < 0.05; their weight is excess similarity.

What it tells us

The network represents pairwise voting agreement that exceeds the vote-preserving null expectation and passes the chosen statistical criterion. It captures more than simply counting same-way votes.

What it does not tell us

An edge is not evidence of friendship, cooperation, ideological similarity, or strategic coordination. It represents a statistical relationship in observed parliamentary voting during the analysed period.

What we see

Across 20 weighted Louvain runs with different fixed random seeds, every run produced 9 communities. Mean pairwise NMI was 0.9623 and median pairwise NMI was 0.9629.

What it tells us

The detected community structure is highly consistent across these runs. Changing the algorithm's random seed generally produces very similar partitions.

What it does not tell us

Stability does not prove that nine communities are the uniquely correct or true division of the network. It shows that this network and this method repeatedly recover similar partitions.

04 / Compared with null

Real Q0.1530

Null mean Q0.0630

Empirical p0.00990

What we see

Representative weighted modularity is Q = 0.1530. Degree-preserving null networks have mean Q = 0.0630, SD = 0.00229, and empirical one-sided p = 0.00990; observed Q lies above the null distribution.

What it tells us

Under the selected baseline, the observed network contains stronger community structure than expected. The null preserves the node set and degree sequence, rewires edges, and randomly permutes observed weights across shuffled edges.

What it does not tell us

This does not explain why communities exist or establish ideology, party strategy, government/opposition relations, or personal cooperation. The conclusion is conditional on the chosen network definition and null model.

05 / Communities and parties

COMMUNITY ↔ PARTYNMI0.6449

Formal party membership and detected voting communities overlap, but they do not define identical partitions.

What we see

NMI between the representative Louvain communities and formal party membership is 0.6449.

What it tells us

Party membership and detected voting communities contain overlapping information, but they do not produce identical partitions. The voting network is not perfectly described by party labels alone.

What it does not tell us

NMI does not explain why particular politicians or parties appear together or apart, nor does it establish that either partition is better or more meaningful. Those differences require direct examination of composition and voting patterns.
